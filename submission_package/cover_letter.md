# Cover letter — Acta Astronautica

Dear Editors,

We submit "Deep-Time Celestial Reference Networks for Reconstructing Terrestrial Orientation" for consideration as an original research paper in Acta Astronautica.

The paper treats a celestial-reference-system problem: how to encode Earth's spin-axis direction at a reference epoch so that it remains reconstructable by a future observer after 10^3–10^8 years, when individual sources have moved, been lost, or been misidentified. Rather than searching for a persistent landmark, we encode orientation relationally — cosines to a redundant source network plus Gram and chirality constraints — and quantify recoverability under source loss, correspondence corruption, observational noise, class-specific secular drift, and unfavorable geometry, using least-squares, Huber, and RANSAC-like estimators in Monte Carlo simulation with explicit seeds and declared scenario assumptions.

The contribution is methodological and astronautical: an integrated treatment of celestial reference-network design, redundant relational encoding, robust reconstruction, source degradation and misidentification, information-cost optimization, and deep-time stress testing — including a nontrivial negative result: at the longest horizons, source-class stability and usability dominate over heterogeneity per se, so homogeneous quasar networks outperformed the tested heterogeneous designs. A direct-axis landmark control separates the algebraic underdetermination of a single relational scalar from the physical fragility of a single landmark. The results bear on the design of extreme-duration reference systems — interstellar probe frames, long-lived archives, and interstellar information architectures — and connect directly to ICRF/Gaia frame practice and to pulsar-map encodings such as Pioneer/Voyager, whose qualitative idea this work turns into a quantitative, falsifiable problem.

All code, simulation outputs, figures, and tables are generated by a single `make all` from the public repository https://github.com/bougtoir/prospective-archaeoastronomy with a fixed random seed; every manuscript number is machine-extracted and audited for traceability; a reference audit accompanies the package. No archival DOI has been minted; the repository is the canonical source.

The manuscript is original, not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors approve submission. Competing interests, funding, CRediT roles, AI-use, and data-availability declarations are included.
