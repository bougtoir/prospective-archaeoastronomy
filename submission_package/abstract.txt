Terrestrial orientation is not permanently tied to any single celestial landmark: stars drift, pulsars fade from the beam line, and any individual reference source can be lost or misidentified over timescales far shorter than the horizons relevant to the longest-lived information systems. This paper formulates the recovery of a stored Earth spin-axis direction as a celestial reference-network reconstruction problem and evaluates it in a three-layer simulation study. Layer 1 is a general synthetic theory: a controlled parameter-space experiment that maps the recoverability surface P(recover | drift, loss, mismatch, geometry, precision, estimator, chirality) for seven reference-network architecture classes. Layer 2 is observation-calibrated validation: real Gaia DR3 bright stars (n = 2400), ATNF pulsars (n = 3470), and ICRF3 quasars (n = 824) are mapped into that parameter space through measured proper motions, catalogue positions and geometry, and a literature-constrained extragalactic stability prior, and the same reconstruction task is run on networks built from the observed populations. Layer 3 separates far-horizon behaviour: the observation-anchored regime is 10^3–10^5 yr, 10^6 yr is reported as an extended physically constrained projection, and 10^7–10^8 yr runs are reported separately as extrapolative stress tests, not as calibrated forecasts. Under the calibrated models a 50-source ICRF3 quasar network achieved P(error < 1 deg) = 1.0 at 10^6 yr, while a single relational landmark still failed almost universally (invalid-solution probability 1.0). Eight pre-declared synthetic conclusions were then classified as confirmed, partially confirmed, reversed, or not testable under observation-based calibration (Section 3.7): redundancy, geometry, chirality control, robust estimation, and single-source fragility survived; quasar dominance and finite-precision relevance were qualified rather than absolute; and heterogeneity was again found not to be automatically beneficial. The central claim is a general recoverability framework whose parameter space can be calibrated with observed celestial source populations, separating physically constrained from extrapolative regimes.
