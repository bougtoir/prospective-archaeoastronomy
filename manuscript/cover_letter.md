# Cover letter — Advances in Space Research

Dear Editors,

I submit "Deep-Time Celestial Reference Networks for Reconstructing Terrestrial Orientation" for consideration as an original research paper in Advances in Space Research.

The paper addresses a celestial reference-frame problem with direct relevance to long-duration space research: how can Earth's spin-axis direction be encoded at a reference epoch so that it remains reconstructable after 10^3–10^8 years, when individual reference sources have drifted, faded, been lost, or been misidentified? This is the same design question that underlies ICRS/ICRF realizations, Gaia's kinematically non-rotating frame, and pulsar-map encodings such as Pioneer/Voyager — recast here for the extreme case in which the frame must be decoded by a future observer rather than maintained by a living organization.

Methodologically, I encode orientation relationally — per-source cosines to north plus optional Gram and chirality (signed-triple-product) constraints — and quantify recoverability under source loss, correspondence corruption, observational noise, class-specific secular drift, and unfavorable geometry, comparing least-squares, Huber, and RANSAC-like estimators in Monte Carlo simulation with fixed seeds and declared scenario assumptions. Seven architecture classes (single landmark, stellar triangles, multi-star, pulsar, quasar/ICRF-like, galaxy/cluster, heterogeneous hybrid) are evaluated across six time horizons.

The main findings are nontrivial: (i) a single relational scalar is algebraically underdetermined — even a direct-axis landmark benchmark shows that fault tolerance, not identifiability, limits minimal designs; (ii) chirality storage removes a discrete catastrophic mirror failure at negligible information cost; (iii) RANSAC-like consensus survives misidentification fractions where least squares collapses; and (iv) — a negative result I report explicitly — under baseline assumptions, heterogeneous networks do NOT outperform equal-size homogeneous ones: source-class stability and usability, not heterogeneity per se, govern deep-time robustness, and homogeneous quasar networks led at the longest horizons. All conclusions are framed as scenario-conditional, and an information-cost–reliability Pareto frontier gives designers an explicit trade space for long-lived reference systems, interstellar probe frames, and deep-space archives.

Reproducibility is integral: the complete pipeline — simulation grid, summary statistics, figures, tables, and every manuscript number — is regenerated by a single `make all` from the public repository https://github.com/bougtoir/prospective-archaeoastronomy with a fixed master seed; manuscript values are machine-extracted and audited for traceability. All equations are native Word equation objects.

I confirm that the manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. Competing interests, funding, CRediT roles, generative-AI use, and data-availability declarations are included.
