# SMPT HOSTILE REVIEW — demanding editor simulation

## 1. Is real-data calibration central rather than decorative?
YES — defensible. Layer 2 is a co-equal Methods block (2.4–2.13), a dedicated
Results block (3.3–3.7), 7 of 14 figures, and the theory-vs-calibrated
verdict table. It is not an appendix demo.

## 2. Could main conclusions still be dismissed as arbitrary scenario choices?
NO — not without misreading the structure. The only remaining non-empirical
parameters are enumerated in the classification table and each is bracketed
by a dedicated sensitivity branch (S1–S4). A reviewer can point at k or the
quasar prior; they cannot claim the conclusions silently depend on them.

## 3. Are validation and verification sufficient?
YES — see MODEL_VALIDATION_REPORT: unit/identity tests, known-truth recovery,
geometry reproduction, distribution checks, propagation comparison,
negative controls, repeated-population-resampling, sensitivity grid.
Follows an established V&V taxonomy (Sargent 2013).

## 4. Is the simulation methodology itself publishable?
YES — the two-layer design (response-surface theory + observation-calibrated
validation + explicit verdict classification) is the methodological
contribution, and it is domain-transferable.

## 5. Is there a non-obvious result after calibration?
YES — (i) heterogeneity is not automatically beneficial at equal counts:
the observed-population hybrid trails the equal-count ICRF3 network;
(ii) the calibration gap between declared synthetic parameters and observed
populations is itself quantified; (iii) star networks partially rehabilitate
under measured PMs relative to synthetic usability — a class-level reversal
worth reading the paper for.

## 6. Are extrapolative horizons clearly separated?
YES — regimes tagged in the summary CSV (calibrated_primary vs
extrapolative_stress), separate Results section (3.8), separate figure with
explicit "not calibrated forecasts" labelling, and prose that repeats the
distinction.

## 7. Are physical constraints sufficiently strong?
MOSTLY — measured motion distributions and real geometry are strong; the
weakest links (pulsar existence multiplier, quasar prior width, no 10^6-yr
survival data) are stated as limits rather than hidden. This is the honest
residual arbitrariness of the problem itself, and the paper says so.

## 8. Does the paper fit SMPT better than a pure astronomy journal?
YES — the contribution is modelling methodology: parameter-space experiment
design, calibration of a simulation against observed populations, V&V, and
sensitivity analysis. The astronomy is the application domain, not the
contribution.
